# Steps 63–65: \(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) persistence, isolated \(\beta_1\), interaction

Step 62 showed \(\beta_2\) alone is a modest non-monotonic +5.6%
hop cost. This isolates \(\beta_1\) / \(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) the same
way, then asks whether \(\beta_1=\beta_2=1\) is more than the sum.

Unit suite unchanged (40/40). Raw: `results/STEP63_EKF_TREND.md`,
`results/scenario3_beta1_sweep.csv`.

Hop-flight comparison uses the Step 61/62 number: **2.03 s**.
Persistence language is the precise one: comparable to, not
reliably longer than, a single hop. The damning number is p25 = 1 s.

## Step 63 — sign persistence of \(e_k-e_{k-1}\) (Kalman, not trigger)

Same placement and Gauss-Markov as Step 61 (seed 20260921, \(N=40\),
\(T=120\) s). Live `ElqtpProtocol` EKF: \(e_k=z_{\tilde{}}-h(\hat{x})\)
(SPEC 2.3 / 2.6). Not \(e_k^{\mathrm{trigger}}\). Directed AUV–AUV
slots (1 560), ticks \(k=2\ldots 120\) (tick 1 has \(e_{k-1}=0\)
from the constructor). Event trigger on, same \(\varepsilon_0=2\),
\(s_{\mathrm{cap}}=2\) as Scenario III, so HELLO / velocity-write
matches the live protocol. \(\beta_1\) does not enter \(e_k\).

Same-sign run of \(\Delta e_k=e_k-e_{k-1}\):

| | mean | median | p25 | p75 | \(\tau_{1/e}\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\mathrm{sign}(\Delta e_k)\) run (s) | 6.70 | **4.0** | **1.0** | 9.0 | **4.04** |
| Step 61 \(\mathrm{sign}(v_{\mathrm{rad}})\) | 8.63 | 3.0 | 1.0 | 10.0 | 10.4 |
| one hop | | **2.03** | | | |

- 26 904 runs. 1514 / 1560 directed pairs flip (97%).
- Ticks: 48.5% \(\Delta e>0\) (the \(\beta_1\) term binds) / 48.7%
  \(\Delta e<0\) / 2.9% zero-or-nan. No preferred direction.
- ACF(1)=0.72, ACF(5)=0.29, ACF(10)=0.06, ACF(20)=−0.05.
  Memory is **shorter** than radial-sign (which still had ACF(10)=0.38).

Median 4.0 s is slightly longer than one hop (2.03 s) — same
nuance as Step 61. What is damning is p25 = 1.0 s: a quarter of
\(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) “trend” runs die in a single tick, before the
packet finishes even one hop. \(\tau_{1/e}=4.0\) s is only two hops
and is *less* persistent than \(c_{\mathrm{drift}}\)’s radial sign.

\(|\Delta e_k|\) itself is tiny on the bulk: median 0.0135 dB,
mean 0.036 dB, p75 0.035 dB. When \(\Delta e>0\),
\(\max(0,\Delta e)\) median is 0.014 dB. Pair (1,2) later in the
window does spike (\(e_k\sim 2\), \(\Delta e\sim 0.7\)–1.3 dB at
\(t=100\)–120); that is the tail, not the typical tick.

**Finding:** the Kalman trend that feeds \(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) is
similarly short-lived — if anything, *shorter* in autocorrelation
than \(v_{\mathrm{rad}}\). The same noise-chasing structure applies
in the link-quality domain.

There is a second, scale reason this is worse than \(c_{\mathrm{drift}}\).
January high-SNR links sit on the BER-floor plateau:
\(c_{\mathrm{lin}}=\ln 1/(1-10^{-9})^{400}\approx 4\times 10^{-7}\).
A typical positive \(\Delta e\) of 0.014 dB with \(\beta_1=1\) makes

```
c_pred = 4e-7 + 1.0 * 0.014 ≈ 0.014
```

about \(3\times 10^{4}\) times \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\). The reward
denominator jumps by four orders of magnitude on a hundredth-of-a-dB
innovation tick. \(\beta_1=0.25\) still gives \(0.0035\) vs
\(4\times 10^{-7}\). Any \(\beta_1>0\) saturates \(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\)
away from \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\) on this field.

## Step 64 — isolate \(\beta_1\): \(\beta_2=0\), sweep \(\beta_1\)

LQARP vs E-LQTP, same 5 seeds, \(N=40\), \(T_{\mathrm{sim}}=600\) s,
Scenario III, \(\beta_2=0\) (the tied-with-LQARP setting from Step 62).
\(\beta_1\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1.0,2.0\}\). 30 runs, all finite, VOID=0.
First-fire stays 0.708; \(O_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\approx 211\).

Mean ± std over 5 seeds.

| config | PDR | \(T_{\mathrm{e2e}}\) (s) | \(E_{\mathrm{bit}}\) | \(O_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\) | hops | vs LQARP hops |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| LQARP | 0.9989 ± 0.0018 | 4.146 ± 0.348 | 1.099e-2 | 0 | 2.046 ± 0.187 | — |
| E-LQTP \(\beta_1=0\), \(\beta_2=0\) | 0.9989 ± 0.0018 | 4.167 ± 0.318 | 1.101e-2 | 211.0 ± 33.8 | 2.050 ± 0.186 | **1.002×** |
| E-LQTP \(\beta_1=0.25\), \(\beta_2=0\) | 0.9983 ± 0.0015 | 6.831 ± 0.288 | 1.721e-2 | 211.2 ± 34.9 | 3.205 ± 0.184 | **1.57×** |
| E-LQTP \(\beta_1=0.5\), \(\beta_2=0\) | 0.9984 ± 0.0018 | 7.128 ± 0.083 | 1.777e-2 | 210.3 ± 33.2 | 3.309 ± 0.156 | 1.62× |
| E-LQTP \(\beta_1=1.0\), \(\beta_2=0\) | 0.9986 ± 0.0014 | 7.041 ± 0.285 | 1.769e-2 | 211.7 ± 34.5 | 3.296 ± 0.235 | **1.61×** |
| E-LQTP \(\beta_1=2.0\), \(\beta_2=0\) | 0.9989 ± 0.0015 | 7.278 ± 0.388 | 1.826e-2 | 211.5 ± 34.7 | 3.405 ± 0.263 | 1.66× |

\(\beta_1=0\) again matches LQARP (and Step 62’s \(\beta_2=0\)).
The first positive value, \(\beta_1=0.25\), already produces
**1.57× hops and 1.65× delay** — essentially Step 60’s 1.59×.
Further increases add only a few more points (1.57 → 1.66).
It does not come back down (unlike \(\beta_2\)). It is a
threshold, not a smooth dose: any \(\beta_1>0\) saturates the
damage, which is exactly what the \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\)-floor
arithmetic predicted.

\(\beta_1=1\) alone: hops 3.296, \(T_{\mathrm{e2e}}\) 7.041.
Step 60 (\(\beta_1=\beta_2=1\)): hops 3.250, \(T_{\mathrm{e2e}}\) 6.961.
**\(\beta_1=1\) alone reproduces (slightly exceeds) Step 60.**

## Step 65 — additive prediction vs Step 60

Using LQARP hops 2.046 as the common baseline:

| effect | hops | vs LQARP |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(\beta_2=1\), \(\beta_1=0\) (Step 62) | 2.161 | **+5.6%** |
| \(\beta_1=1\), \(\beta_2=0\) (Step 64) | 3.296 | **+61.1%** |
| additive prediction | 2.046+0.115+1.250 = **3.411** | **+66.7%** |
| Step 60 \(\beta_1=\beta_2=1\) | **3.250** | **+58.9%** |

Step 60 is **below** the additive sum and **within a couple of
percent of \(\beta_1\) alone** (3.250 vs 3.296). There is no
super-additive / multiplicative explosion from putting a noisy
numerator (\(c_{\mathrm{drift}}\)) and a noisy denominator
(\(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\)) together. If anything the pair is slightly
sub-additive. \(\beta_1\)’s individual effect already accounts for
Step 60.

A full \(\beta_1\times\beta_2\) grid is not required to explain the
headline. The story is already clear from the two one-factor sweeps.

## How to write this

Step 61 nuance, applied to both signals: persistence is
**comparable to, not reliably longer than**, a single hop. p25 = 1 s
is the sentence that matters.

\(c_{\mathrm{drift}}\) (Steps 61–62): the closing-range assumption
fails; no \(\beta_2\) wins; the cost of \(\beta_2=1\) is +6% hops.

\(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) (Steps 63–64): the Kalman \(\Delta e_k\)
assumption fails the same way, and is worse in two respects —
shorter autocorrelation, and a scale mismatch with floored
\(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\) that makes any \(\beta_1>0\) swamp the
denominator. \(\beta_1=0.25\) already reproduces Step 60.

Step 60’s 1.59× is \(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) at an unswept
\(\beta_1=1\), not \(c_{\mathrm{drift}}\) and not a
\(\beta_1\times\beta_2\) interaction.

The paper can say, honestly: on this January high-SNR eddy field,
the event trigger still cuts control traffic to ~0.5× periodic, and
neither drift-awareness nor one-step innovation-trend awareness
improves the data plane. \(\beta_1=\beta_2=0\) (E-LQTP trigger on,
LQARP reward) is the configuration that matches LQARP hops and
keeps the \(O_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\) win. \(\beta_1=\beta_2=1\) was a
diagnostic-stage placeholder, not a validated operating point.

## Checkpoint

- \(\mathrm{sign}(\Delta e_k)\): median 4 s, p25 1 s, \(\tau_{1/e}=4\) s.
  Same structural problem as \(v_{\mathrm{rad}}\), shorter memory.
- Any \(\beta_1>0\) at \(\beta_2=0\) ≈ Step 60’s hop cost.
- Step 60 ≈ \(\beta_1\) alone; no super-additive interaction.
- Full \(\beta_1\times\beta_2\) grid not needed for the headline.
