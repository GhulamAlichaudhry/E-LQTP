# Steps 66–67: scale-consistent \(\beta_1\), then the operating-point call

Steps 63–65 showed that \(O(1)\) \(\beta_1\) never acted as a weight:
\(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\)’s BER-floor plateau is \(\approx 4\times 10^{-7}\), a
typical positive Kalman \(\Delta e_k\) is \(0.014\) dB, and
\(\beta_1\cdot\max(0,\Delta e)\) at \(\beta_1=0.25\) is already
\(\sim 10^{4}\times c_{\mathrm{lin}}\). That is a unit/scale mismatch
in the additive structure of SPEC 2.6, not proof that trend-awareness
is conceptually unsound. This is the cheap check: put \(\beta_1\) on
the scale that can actually blend with the floor, and see whether any
value there recovers a real data-plane win.

Unit suite unchanged (40/40). Raw:
`results/scenario3_beta1_scaled_sweep.csv`.
\(\beta_2=0\) throughout. Same 5 seeds, \(N=40\),
\(T_{\mathrm{sim}}=600\) s, Scenario III.

### Does hop count stay near LQARP?

Yes, on the two values that actually blend rather than replace
\(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\):

- \(\beta_1=10^{-7}\): 0.998× hops, delay and energy inside the
  LQARP error bar. Under-weighted, as the \(0.0035\times\)
  arithmetic predicted.
- \(\beta_1=10^{-6}\): 0.988× hops, \(T_{\mathrm{e2e}}\) 4.090 vs
  4.146, \(E_{\mathrm{bit}}\) 1.083e-2 vs 1.099e-2, PDR 1.0000.
  This is the only cell that *looks* slightly favorable.

At the theoretically correct blend (\(\beta_1=10^{-5}\),
correction \(\approx 0.35\times c_{\mathrm{lin}}\)) hops are already
**+3.2%** and delay / energy are up. From there the cost is
monotonic: +7.2% at \(10^{-4}\), +13.9% at \(10^{-3}\), and Step 64
takes over at \(O(1)\) (1.57× at \(\beta_1=0.25\)).

Hops staying near LQARP at \(10^{-7}\)–\(10^{-6}\) is exactly the
signature of a term that now blends. The \(O(1)\) “any
\(\beta_1>0\) saturates at 1.6×” threshold is gone. Scale was the
bug.

\(\beta_1=10^{-6}\) is **2 seeds down, 3 seeds up**. The −1.2%
mean is a large win on two placements offset by losses on the other
three. Against LQARP’s hop std of 0.187, \(0.025\) hops is
\(0.13\sigma\). Delay and energy have the same mixed-sign pattern.

PDR \(1.0000\) at \(10^{-6}\) and \(10^{-5}\) is four recovered
packets (seed 23: 705→708; seed 24: 752→753; both
`LOST_NODE_EXIT` 3+1 → 0). That is the same noise scale as Step 64’s
PDR band (0.9983–0.9989). It is not a harder-condition gain: the
baseline is already 0.9989. And \(10^{-5}\) shares the PDR ceiling
while costing hops, so the ceiling is not a \(\beta_1=10^{-6}\)
signature.

\(\beta_1=10^{-5}\) is the first value that *reliably* moves hops,
and it moves them the wrong way (4 of 5 seeds worse).

No cell in this range shows a clear, favorable difference from
LQARP. The one that looks slightly better is seed-sign-inconsistent
and well inside the error bar.

## Step 67 — no refine

Step 67’s refine (half / double a beneficial point, same 5 seeds)
is for a value that beats LQARP, not for parity or a \(0.13\sigma\)
wobble. **No refine is run.** A local-optimum hunt around
\(10^{-6}\) would be fitting seed noise.

What the range *does* show, which is the other half of the Step 67
question:

- \(\beta_1=10^{-7}\) is under-weighted: indistinguishable from
  \(\beta_1=0\) / LQARP in both directions.
- \(\beta_1=10^{-6}\) is still under-weighted in the routing-decision
  sense: it can flip a handful of next-hops (the mixed seed signs)
  but not in a preferred direction.
- \(\beta_1=10^{-5}\) is the first scale with a consistent effect,
  and that effect is a hop / delay / energy cost.
- There is therefore **no scale of \(\beta_1\) that both (a) avoids
  swamping \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and (b) has enough influence to
  help.** The window between “no effect” and “starts hurting” is
  one decade, and the first decade that bites is already
  unfavorable.

That is a different finding from “\(\beta_1=0\) is best because
trend-awareness doesn’t help conceptually.” The mechanism works
exactly as written: once the additive term is large enough to
compete with the floor, it changes NextHop. The signal it is
weighting — one-step Kalman \(\Delta e_k\), median \(0.014\) dB,
p25 same-sign persistence **1 s**, \(\tau_{1/e}=4\) s (Step 63) —
is too small and too short-lived, on this January high-SNR eddy
field, to be a useful routing feature at any scale. BER-floor
saturation plus hop-scale (and shorter) innovation memory are
site / field properties, not a coding bug.

The \(O(1)\) 1.59× of Step 60 remains a scale-mismatch bug. Fixing
the scale removes the blow-up and does not reveal a hidden win
underneath it.

## How to write this

Do **not** write “trend-awareness doesn’t help, therefore
\(\beta_1=0\).” Write the two-part finding:

1. SPEC 2.6’s additive form \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}+\beta_1\max(0,\Delta e)\)
   is scale-inconsistent on a BER-floored high-SNR field. A
   numerically comparable \(\beta_1\) is \(10^{-5}\)–\(10^{-6}\),
   not \(O(1)\). At \(O(1)\), \(\beta_1\) is an on/off switch that
   replaces \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and reproduces Step 60.
2. After putting \(\beta_1\) on that comparable scale, no value
   both blends and improves PDR / hops / delay / energy. The
   one-step innovation is too weak and too brief (median 0.014 dB,
   p25 persistence 1 s) to inform NextHop. That is a citable
   statement about this site’s SNR field, not a claim that every
   conceivable trend feature is useless.

The paper’s operating point on Scenario III can now be stated
without the “we never checked the right scale” caveat:
**E-LQTP trigger on, \(\beta_1=\beta_2=0\)** (LQARP reward). That
keeps the \(O_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\) win (\(\approx 211\) vs periodic
413) and matches LQARP’s data plane. \(\beta_1=\beta_2=1\) was a
diagnostic-stage placeholder.

A redesigned \(c_{\mathrm{pred}}\) (relative form, or a trend
feature with longer memory than one hop) is future work, not a
claim of this sweep.

## Checkpoint

- Hop count stays at LQARP at \(\beta_1=10^{-7}\) and \(10^{-6}\)
  (0.998× / 0.988×). The term now blends. Scale was the Step 64 bug.
- No \(\beta_1\) in \(\{10^{-7},\ldots,10^{-3}\}\) shows a clear
  favorable difference. \(10^{-6}\) is mixed-sign \(0.13\sigma\).
- First consistent move is \(\beta_1=10^{-5}\) (**+3.2% hops**),
  then +7% / +14% as the term starts to replace \(c_{\mathrm{lin}}\).
- No refine. No scale both avoids swamping and helps.
- Finding: this Kalman \(\Delta e_k\) is too small and too
  short-lived to usefully inform routing at any scale, given
  BER-floor saturation and 1 s p25 persistence.
- Operating point, now checked at the right scale:
  \(\beta_1=\beta_2=0\), trigger on.
